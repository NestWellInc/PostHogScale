# Foremanly Systems — First-Revenue Acquisition Playbook

**Purpose:** turn the current offer/operations stack into qualified conversations and first verified external revenue without paid acquisition, fabricated proof, or premature Shopify publication.

## Commercial objective
Prioritize the first 3 genuine paid cleanup engagements over expanding the SKU count. These jobs should produce real evidence about buyer type, scope, delivery time, revisions, channel economics, objections, and repeat need.

## Primary ICPs
Target organizations or operators with a visible reason to maintain CSV/spreadsheet data and a plausible bounded cleanup need.

### Tier A — highest fit
1. Small ecommerce operators managing product/catalog CSVs.
2. Small agencies or consultants regularly receiving client spreadsheets/exports.
3. Small B2B teams maintaining CRM/contact exports.
4. Operations/admin contractors who routinely reconcile multiple CSV/XLSX files.

### Tier B — conditional fit
- small businesses preparing a migration/import into a named platform;
- analysts/virtual assistants inheriting inconsistent operational spreadsheets;
- organizations consolidating exports from multiple sources.

Avoid targeting regulated/sensitive-data-heavy use cases until handling suitability is explicitly established. Do not solicit work involving credentials, payment-card data, medical records, government identifiers, or other data that exceeds the approved handling model.

## Qualification signals
A prospect is stronger when at least two are observable or stated:
- repeated CSV/XLSX import/export workflow;
- duplicate/contact/catalog cleanup pain;
- migration or platform import project;
- multiple files/sources that need normalization;
- manual spreadsheet cleanup consuming staff time;
- inconsistent dates, IDs, names, prices, statuses, or headers;
- recent request/job post mentioning data cleanup, formatting, deduplication, CSV, Excel, CRM import, Shopify import, or spreadsheet organization.

Do not invent pain points from a company name alone.

## Offer routing
Use the smallest offer that honestly matches the need:
- unclear problem / buyer wants findings only → **$19 CSV Data Quality Audit**;
- one bounded CSV/spreadsheet cleanup → **$49 CSV & Spreadsheet Cleanup**;
- Shopify product CSV → **$59 Shopify Product CSV Preflight**;
- CRM/contact export → **$59 CRM Contact CSV Cleanup**;
- destination-spec preparation → **$79 Import-Ready CSV Preparation**;
- multiple files requiring normalization → **$99 Multi-File Cleanup & Normalization**.

Do not force a prospect into a higher-priced SKU. Rescope only when the actual files/rules exceed the listed boundary.

## Free acquisition sequence
Until the owned storefront clears publication QA, prioritize channels that can produce a scoped conversation without requiring paid ads or new subscriptions.

1. **Marketplace demand capture:** use the prepared Fiverr and Upwork core-cleanup assets when authenticated publication is available. Start with one focused listing per marketplace.
2. **High-intent opportunity response:** respond only to public/authorized requests where the buyer explicitly needs CSV/spreadsheet cleanup, deduplication, formatting, import preparation, or related work. Tailor the response to the stated problem; do not mass-post generic pitches.
3. **Warm/network outreach:** when an authorized contact already knows the owner/business, use a short problem-specific note and offer the $19 audit or bounded cleanup when relevant.
4. **Organic proof distribution:** reuse synthetic portfolio evidence to explain a concrete cleanup pattern. Clearly label examples synthetic; CTA should invite a scoped audit/cleanup conversation, not promise outcomes.

## Outreach quality rule
Every outbound message must pass all five checks:
1. **Relevant:** references a real, observable need or explicit request.
2. **Specific:** names the applicable CSV/spreadsheet problem rather than generic “data services.”
3. **Bounded:** proposes a defined next step, usually an audit or scoped cleanup.
4. **Truthful:** no invented client count, testimonials, savings, accuracy rate, turnaround history, or platform acceptance claim.
5. **Low-pressure:** no fake urgency, misleading personalization, or repeated unsolicited follow-up.

## Conversation → order workflow
1. Confirm file type, approximate rows/files, desired output, and problem.
2. Identify whether duplicate removal, missing-value handling, destination mapping, or sensitive data requires explicit rules.
3. Route to the smallest matching offer.
4. State what is included/excluded before work begins.
5. Preserve the original and use the canonical intake/QA workflow.
6. Record channel, quoted price, actual platform fee, delivery time, revision time, and outcome after the job.

## First-3-jobs learning ledger
For each genuine external job record:
- date;
- acquisition channel;
- buyer type / use case;
- SKU or custom scope;
- gross price;
- platform/payment fees;
- verified net cash;
- rows/files processed;
- delivery minutes/hours;
- revision minutes/hours;
- main issue categories;
- objection before purchase;
- reason won/lost if known;
- repeat/referral signal;
- scope surprise or rescope trigger.

After 3 paid jobs, calculate net dollars per delivery hour and compare channels/use cases. Do not change the whole pricing ladder from one anomalous order.

## Acquisition metrics
Track weekly:
- qualified opportunities found;
- tailored responses/outreach sent;
- replies;
- scoped conversations;
- quotes/offers sent;
- jobs won;
- verified gross/net cash;
- response → conversation rate;
- conversation → paid rate;
- median delivery time;
- revision rate.

Do not optimize vanity metrics such as raw impressions or message volume before qualified-response data exists.

## Stop / pivot rules
- If a channel produces 20 qualified, tailored attempts with zero meaningful replies, inspect targeting/message/credibility before increasing volume.
- If conversations occur but no purchases, inspect scope, trust/proof, pricing, and payment friction.
- If purchases occur but net dollars/hour are poor, tighten scope or adjust channel-specific pricing after enough evidence.
- If buyers repeatedly request the same adjacent service, log it; create a new SKU only after repeat evidence rather than one request.

## Current execution dependency
The offer stack, intake design, synthetic proof, marketplace listing frameworks, and digital packages already exist. The main bottleneck is no longer product ideation. It is clearing authenticated publication/checkout/intake/delivery gates and then generating qualified demand. Future workers should not create additional acquisition documents unless they implement a materially different channel or resolve an observed conversion problem.
